Metrology

MicroTopos reports areal surface-texture parameters in the style of ISO 25178. The implementation follows the standard’s definitions but is not certified. Every report states the filters, units and calibration status the numbers depend on.

From frames to heights

Stage Method

Sweep

frames between the detected start and end of the focus movement; a turn back through focus (reversal) is detected and only one direction is used

Registration

sub-pixel translation between consecutive frames (ECC), chained, so small stage drift is removed

Focus measure

difference-of-Gaussians band-pass energy per pixel in a window matched to the objective’s resolution

Depth

a Gaussian (log-parabola) fit through the five frames around each pixel’s focus peak, on the actual z values

Confidence

from the peak’s prominence and the noise of the focus curve; unreliable pixels are left empty (NaN)

Smoothing

confidence-weighted guided filter with outlier rejection; edges are preserved

All-in-focus

Laplacian-pyramid fusion with soft weights, at full camera resolution

Surface preparation

Before any parameter is computed, the height map is prepared:

  1. Form removal: a least-squares polynomial (order 1 = plane, 2 = quadric; step 6) is subtracted.

  2. S-filter: a Gaussian low-pass, ISO 16610-61, σ = 0.1874 λs, removes noise below the optical resolution. The default λs follows the objective’s resolution.

  3. L-filter (optional): a Gaussian high-pass with cut-off λc removes waviness.

Only valid pixels are used throughout. If fewer than 30 % of the pixels have a reliable depth, the parameters are withheld and the report says why.

When a value is withheld

Every report states the surface operator it used (form removal, S- and L-filter, valid fraction) before the numbers, and each group says why a value shows "—":

  • Height, material-ratio, spatial and spectral parameters need at least 30 % valid depth pixels.

  • Sdr needs heights in µm.

  • Sal and Str are withheld when the autocorrelation has too few valid pixel pairs in some direction, for example with patterned or clustered missing pixels. An unsupported lag is unknown, not zero correlation. Std is withheld for a flat surface or a spectrum without a dominant direction.

  • Gaussian filters are applied only when the cut-off spans at least 4 pixels. Below that, a sampled Gaussian does not have the standard’s 50 % transmission: it is 0.89 at 2 px and 0.56 at 3 px, against 0.502 at 4 px. The default λs (the optical cut-off) is skipped with a note when the pixels are too coarse for it. A value you entered yourself is refused with a warning.

  • Form removal order 0 removes the mean only, 1 a plane, 2 a quadratic, 3 a cubic.

  • Smr1 and Smr2 are where the material-ratio curve crosses the core limits. On a flat plateau (tied heights) this gives the same answer as on a slightly perturbed one.

  • Texture (GLCM) and grains come from the all-in-focus image, not from the heights, and are labelled image-based. Watershed regions are not verified metallurgical grains.

Note
 Grain statistics come from the image, not the height, and depend on how visible the boundaries are: an etched metallographic section works best. Check them with Overlay grain boundaries on the All-in-focus tab.

Limits you should know

  • Optical limits. Lateral resolution is limited by the objective (about 0.61 λ/NA) and by the camera’s Bayer sensor and MJPEG compression. Height resolution is a fraction of the depth of field and depends on the texture.

  • Focus breathing (magnification changing with focus) is not corrected. The effect is about 1 pixel at the image edges in far-defocused frames.

  • Manual Z. A hand sweep gives relative focus progression: an uneven knob speed distorts it, a different speed gives a different scale, and its direction is unknown unless you state it, so the relief can be inverted. A knob travel stated for a sweep calibrates that sweep only, assuming an even speed. Only a measured Z (an encoder or motorised drive) removes this limit.

  • Textureless or mirror-like areas give no focus signal and are left empty. A light etch, oblique lighting or a higher-NA objective helps.

  • Accuracy on real samples has not yet been validated against a certified reference. The figures on the verification list come from simulation ground truth and from real camera texture with a known synthetic relief.
