= Metrology
:description: How MicroTopos computes height maps and ISO 25178-style surface parameters, and what each number means.

MicroTopos reports areal surface-texture parameters in the style of ISO 25178. The implementation follows the standard's definitions but is *not certified*. Every report states the filters, units and calibration status the numbers depend on.

== From frames to heights

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Stage | Method
| Sweep | frames between the detected start and end of the focus movement; a turn back through focus (reversal) is detected and only one direction is used
| Registration | sub-pixel translation between consecutive frames (ECC), chained, so small stage drift is removed
| Focus measure | difference-of-Gaussians band-pass energy per pixel in a window matched to the objective's resolution
| Depth | a Gaussian (log-parabola) fit through the five frames around each pixel's focus peak, on the actual z values
| Confidence | from the peak's prominence and the noise of the focus curve; unreliable pixels are left empty (NaN)
| Smoothing | confidence-weighted guided filter with outlier rejection; edges are preserved
| All-in-focus | Laplacian-pyramid fusion with soft weights, at full camera resolution
|===

== Surface preparation

Before any parameter is computed, the height map is prepared:

. *Form removal*: a least-squares polynomial (order 1 = plane, 2 = quadric; step 6) is subtracted.
. *S-filter*: a Gaussian low-pass, ISO 16610-61, σ = 0.1874 λ~s~, removes noise below the optical resolution. The default λ~s~ follows the objective's resolution.
. *L-filter* (optional): a Gaussian high-pass with cut-off λ~c~ removes waviness.

Only valid pixels are used throughout. If fewer than 30 % of the pixels have a reliable depth, the parameters are withheld and the report says why.

== Parameters

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Parameter | Unit | Meaning
3+| *Height*
| Sa | z | arithmetic mean of the absolute heights
| Sq | z | root-mean-square height
| Sp, Sv, Sz | z | maximum peak height, maximum pit depth, and their sum
| Ssk | – | skewness: negative for a plateau with pits, positive for peaks on a flat
| Sku | – | kurtosis: 3 for a Gaussian surface, larger for sharp peaks or pits
3+| *Hybrid*
| Sdr | % | developed interfacial area ratio: extra true area over the projected area, from two triangles per pixel cell. Shown only when z is in µm.
3+| *Functional (material ratio curve)*
| Sk | z | core height, from the 40 % secant of least slope on the material ratio curve
| Spk | z | reduced peak height: the peaks above the core, which wear off first
| Svk | z | reduced valley depth: the valleys below the core, which hold lubricant
| Smr1, Smr2 | % | material ratios at the top and bottom of the core
3+| *Spatial*
| Sal | µm | fastest decay length of the autocorrelation to 0.2
| Str | – | texture aspect ratio: near 1 isotropic, near 0 strongly directional. Undefined, and not shown, when the autocorrelation does not decay to 0.2 in some direction.
| Std | ° | texture direction (lay), from the angular power spectrum
3+| *Other*
| σ~inst~ | z | repeatability from two sweeps at the same place: std(Δz)/√2
| PSD slope β, fractal dimension | – | log-log slope of the radial power spectrum; D = (8 − β)/2, given only when 2 < β < 4
| GLCM contrast, homogeneity, correlation | – | Haralick texture of the all-in-focus image, 64 grey levels
| Grains, area, max Feret, circularity | –, µm², µm | marker-controlled (h-minima) watershed on the all-in-focus image; grains touching the border are excluded
| Mean lineal intercept, ASTM E112 G | µm, – | Heyn intercept method on the grain boundaries; G = −3.2877 − 6.6439 log~10~(ℓ in mm)
|===

_z_ is µm after Z calibration, otherwise focus frames. Lengths in µm are *nominal* until the lateral scale is calibrated.

NOTE: Grain statistics come from the image, not the height, and depend on how visible the boundaries are: an etched metallographic section works best. Check them with *Overlay grain boundaries* on the All-in-focus tab.

== Limits you should know

* *Optical limits.* Lateral resolution is limited by the objective (about 0.61 λ/NA) and by the camera's Bayer sensor and MJPEG compression. Height resolution is a fraction of the depth of field and depends on the texture.
* *Focus breathing* (magnification changing with focus) is not corrected. The effect is about 1 pixel at the image edges in far-defocused frames.
* *Manual Z.* Heights scale with the knob speed until calibrated, and an uneven speed distorts them. Shapes are right; the scale is what calibration fixes.
* *Textureless or mirror-like areas* give no focus signal and are left empty. A light etch, oblique lighting or a higher-NA objective helps.
* *Accuracy on real samples* has not yet been validated against a certified reference. The figures on the xref:development.adoc#verification[verification] list come from simulation ground truth and from real camera texture with a known synthetic relief.
